# QuadShot — FPV Flight Sandbox

3D FPV drone flight sandbox in Godot, growing into an action flight game. (Game title: **QuadShot** — one o; the repo folder keeps its historical `QuadShoot` spelling.) **The flight model is the product** — everything serves the human-in-the-loop tuning cycle. Milestone 0 spec: [FPV-SANDBOX-HANDOFF.md](FPV-SANDBOX-HANDOFF.md); everything after: [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md); the M6 gameplay model (living-theater campaign) is designed in [GAMEPLAY-DESIGN.md](GAMEPLAY-DESIGN.md) — a living doc + decision log, **append-only** (history is never pruned). Read the relevant one before big changes.

## Running & verifying

- Godot 4.7 stable (standard/GDScript build, portable): `C:\Tools\Godot\Godot_v4.7-stable_win64_console.exe` (use the `_console` exe from CLI so output is visible).
- **[TESTING.md](TESTING.md) is the run-everything manual** — every scene, every bench, the 20 checks, and above all how to WATCH a bench with a window instead of reading its numbers. Both watchable benches take a name filter (`tools\watch_matchups.cmd screamer`), which turns "drop --headless and look at it" from half an hour into a minute; a filtered run is a LOOK and can never write an artifact or fail a board.
- Run the game: `<exe> --path .` — boots the **flyable menu tower** (B5): fly through a floor's lit window and out the far side to launch that leaf (START RUN / FLY FREE / CITY / DEV ROOM / AIM DRILL / QUIT); with no controller connected the menu falls back to a side-view keyboard mode (↑/↓ + Enter, Esc quits). Boot the game scene directly: `<exe> --path . scenes/main.tscn`
- Run the **dev room** testbed: `<exe> --path . scenes/dev_map.tscn` — a big sandbox map that mirrors main's wiring and gets a specimen of every game element as they're added (shooting range, city block, slalom, tunnel, platforms, crash wall, a **Falx** specimen in open sky — boom-and-zoom, the one enemy you bait rather than chase — a **Screamer** specimen further out (EW escort: no weapon at all, but a jam field that fades with distance — fly toward it and the feed breaks up, the gun director goes quiet, the missile refuses to lock and the flak fuse degrades to contact-only; it also **cloaks** — `resources/cloak.gdshader`, a screen-space refraction whose strength IS the jam level, so it is invisible at range and a Predator-style heat haze up close, dropping to a solid hull for 0.3 s on every hit) — and a **procedural city** — a `CityLayout` (`scripts/environment/city_layout.gd`) placing seeded `WorldBuilding`s (`scripts/environment/world_building.gd` → `BuildingGenerator`) on a road/block grid: per-building footprint/height, four-sided windows, setback tiers, a scaffold crown at the top, empty lots; standing beside the solid placeholder city boxes it will replace. Headless checks: `tests/world_building_check.gd`, `tests/city_layout_check.gd` — procgen checks, run on demand rather than as part of TESTING.md's 27-check board).
- Run a **composed sortie** (M6's bridge, Iteration 12): `<exe> --path . scenes/sortie.tscn` (`-- --seed N --node N` picks one, `--fresh` starts a new war, `--no-persist` leaves the save alone; it prints its briefing before you arm). **It is a campaign, not a level**: the result is priced back into the war (`WarSim.apply_sortie` — every kill dents the node even if you die, P2.q4), the war ticks, and the state saves to `user://war.save` (`WarSave`, F4's portable file — `var_to_str` and **never JSON**, which would lose every StringName and turn `rng_state` into a float). The first scene whose contents come from the **war-sim** rather than from a level: it generates a theater, runs `SortieComposer.compose()` on a node, and `SortieRunner` builds exactly what the spec describes — layered garrison, objective structures, and reserves that arrive because of what you *did* (`objective_damaged`), not because the last wave died. You **fly your own ingress** (A6, 2026-08-03): the pilot is put down on the deck 140-195 m out on the spec's own `approach.bearing_deg`, facing the target, and the approach is theirs — which is what makes the concentric rings mean anything, since enemies engage on their distance to the PLAYER and a pilot starting in the middle was outside every ring at once. The range is `SortieRunner.ingress_range()`, a remap of the composer's fiction-unit `ingress_m` (400 m desert → 150 m city) onto the band this arena can host, ORDER PRESERVED — the runner is the only place a spec becomes a Node3D, so it is the only honest place for fiction to become world units. It must stay above `EGRESS_RADIUS` and below `sortie.gd`'s 220 m signal warning, and `sortie_check` asserts both against the files they live in. A **strike** ends on **egress** (flatten the hot-white structures, then fly back out past 105 m); a **dogfight** ends when the field and its reserves are clear. **All seven archetypes fly** (2026-08-01): strike, dogfight, sead, strike_cap, decapitation, interdiction, raid — an archetype IS its objective count, reserve fraction and trigger, all data in `SortieComposer`, so opening the five cost one new firing site: `detected` (`SortieRunner._announce_yourself`, fired by your first kill or first touch of the objective), without which SEAD, Strike-CAP and the Raid would have held a slice of their garrison forever and been quietly EASIER than the node they came from. `sortie_check` scans the runner's source so that every `TRIGGER_ON` value must have a firing site — the only kind of assertion that can catch a trigger nobody wrote a scenario for. The **HQ raid is shielded** until the command network is broken (P1.5): the tick engine always enforced that for its own proxy, and `WarView` now enforces it for the player, which mattered the moment the Raid became flyable.
- Run the **war room** (M6's face, Iteration 13 / P1.8): `<exe> --path . scenes/war_room.tscn` (`-- --seed N`, `--no-persist`, `--fresh`) — the theater as a **hex table**: prism height = garrison, green yours / red theirs / dim out of strike range / slate = an archetype the slice cannot build, an amber wall on the front line and thin plates on supply links. Everything it draws is derived by `WarView` (`scripts/warroom/war_view.gd`), which is pure and headless-checkable, so the renderer (`hex_table.gd`) owns geometry and **no facts**; the room adds **zero fields** to `user://war.save`. The **inspection card** is the first place P1.3's fog has ever reached a human: it runs `compose_briefing` (what intel believes), never `compose` (what is there), so detail degrades exact → families → the abstract strength the war-sim keeps, and it is rendered as TEXT by `WarView.card_lines` so a headless check can assert that a stale card names no unit at all. Weather carries an **exact 1-tick forecast** — `WarSim.weather_after` gives weather its own dice, seeded per (theater seed, node id, tick) rather than drawn from the war's shared stream (C.q4), because a forecast taken from the shared stream would mean **looking at the map moved the war**. **The room OWNS the campaign turn** (C.q2, phase 3): ENTER launches the selected node, `WarLaunch` carries (node, seed, persist) out and the result back, and `WarDebrief.resolve` prices it in and ticks — because P1.8's sequence puts the tick where the map is. Consequences: **death ends the sortie** (no respawn; the pilot leaves the roster, the kills still dent — P5.4's "redeploy from Home Airbase", which is what v2.02 was waiting for), and **quitting mid-sortie loses the sortie**, which IS P1.q4's *exit without save*. `scenes/sortie.tscn` keeps a standalone leg that resolves the war itself, because TESTING.md's repro command depends on it. Reachable from the menu tower's **WAR ROOM** floor. **The tick plays out as map movement** (P1.8's "the theater being alive"): the room snapshots the state before resolving, `WarDiff.between` compares the two afterwards, and `HexTable.play_changes` animates the difference — heights move, ownership cross-fades and flashes, borders redraw last. `WarSim` was deliberately NOT taught to emit events: diffing costs nothing, keeps the one module whose purity is load-bearing for determinism and the save exactly as pure, and produces an animation that cannot disagree with the state because it is derived from it. **Roster + hangar** (`scripts/warroom/hangar.gd`): pilots as marks (a count, not named pilots — C.q7 keeps the save shape frozen) and the airframe pick, which rides `MenuLaunch.frame_id` — the same static the menu tower writes and `FlightController` reads, never a second mechanism, and `war_room_check` asserts the hangar's frame list against the tower's so the two cannot drift. Check: `tests/war_room_check.gd`.
- Run the **scale yard** (PLAN-FULL-SCALE phase 2 / GAMEPLAY-DESIGN V10, branch `full-scale`): `<exe> --path . scenes/scale_map.tscn` — a neon-greybox world whose only job is telling the pilot **how big they are**, and the venue for the **size ladder**: press **1 / 2 / 3** to swap between **Kestrel** (0.28 m, 0.65 kg, TWR 4.5 — the original quad, untouched), **Condor** (1.2 m, 32 kg, TWR 12) and **Roc** (3.0 m, 500 kg, TWR 12) *in place, on the pad, without a scene reload* — because the comparison only says something if the world does not change between the two flights. All three are parked on the apron at true size beside a 1.75 m person. `FrameSwitcher` (`scripts/environment/frame_switcher.gd`) drives it; `FlightController.swap_frame` + `_apply_frame_geometry` rebuild the airframe's collider, body, motors and lens from `FlightConfig.body_m`/`arm_length`/`fpv_offset`, so **one drone scene is any size in the roster**. `fpv_offset` is authored per frame and NEVER derived: at 0.28 m the lens sits inside the hull and the near plane clips it away, at 3 m the same relative offset buries the camera in a solid airframe — scaling a camera OFFSET is not scaling a camera POSITION. It exists because the vast desert could not: *"the desert is not good to feel the change"*, and that is right, because **a dune has no known size**. Scale is not a property a world has, it is a COMPARISON a world offers — so every object here is built at a **real measured size** (1.75 m person, 4.4 m car in a 2.5x5 m bay, 12.19 m container, 8.5 m house, 105x68 m football pitch, 37.6 m airliner beside a 70.6 m widebody, 45 m runway, 45 m pylons at 220 m spacing, 135 m turbines, 150 m mast, 32 m bridge clearance, buildings labelled in FLOORS, altitude comb at 10/25/50/100/200/400 m, ground grid 10 m minor / 100 m major). The human-scale objects stand on an **arc at a common 45 m range**, because down a straight line the far end looks smaller for the wrong reason. **Nothing here is ever scaled by S** — a ruler that gets scaled stops being a ruler, which makes this the one map immune to the world scale. `ScaleYard` (`scripts/environment/scale_yard.gd`) builds it all through `BoxBatcher`; labels are `GlowText3D` billboarded to face the pilot. It carries **no `LookController` deliberately** — that would re-apply the shared look config's `fog_density = 0.006` and swallow everything past 700 m, which is fatal for a map about comparing near objects with far ones.
- Run the **city map**: `<exe> --path . scenes/city_map.tscn` — a dedicated flyable city (`scenes/environment/city.tscn` = clean environment + a large `CityLayout`, no testbed clutter), reusing main's wiring with the combat specimens stripped. The proving ground for procgen breadth (out of the dev room, v1.53) and the base for interior generation. **12x15 = 180 blocks since 2026-08-14** (L13 phase 0.2), about 550x750 m: the size is a budget decision taken from `city_load_bench`, which measures the generator's cost as FLAT per block (~4800 triangles, ~30 meshes) from 20 blocks to 400.
- **THERE IS NO SCALED CITY, AND THERE MUST NOT BE ONE** (user's ruling, 2026-08-15). One was built at 10.714x on the reasoning that a 3 m aircraft should thread streets the way a 0.28 m one does, and the premise was rejected on sight: *"the city should not scale with our new scaling, it should scale to the real world size we are trying to achieve."* **The world is real human scale; a scaled world is exactly what V.q10 closed.** The heavy frame's relationship to a city is not acrobatic threading but *"an extreme flyby at the main route, something that will clearly demonstrate how fast and heavy it is"* — a speed demonstration through a real-sized city, not a bigger city. What survives is `CityLayout.world_scale`, a multiplier over every hard-coded length in that generator (default 1.0, nothing shipped moves), kept as a **regression guard**: building the scaled city is what revealed that the generator never honoured `block_size` at all — the footprint law, the curb, the streetlights and the trees were raw metres, so raising `block_size` alone still strands 16-to-34-metre buildings in oversized blocks. `city_load_bench`'s last row exercises it.
- Run a **pilot-in-the-loop drill** (task 8): `<exe> --path . scenes/drill.tscn -- --drill hold_tilt` (`--list` names them, `--drill rotor_out` is the other). **The human IS the reading** — their ask: *"i want to create an instrument that will put ME as the pilot of a reading you can run and test... you tell me the situation, what you want me to do, and then read the result and compare to your assertions."* A drill is a stated situation, task and success condition (all on screen before you arm) plus named measures, all data in `DrillBook` (`scripts/drills/drill_book.gd`); `DrillMeasures` reduces samples to numbers and is the instrument's ONLY arithmetic, which is what lets `drill_check` feed it synthetic flights. **The agent's prediction is committed to git BEFORE the flight** — one JSON per drill under `drills/predictions/`, so `git log -1 --format=%ct` dates that prediction alone — and `DrillCompare` REFUSES to grade at all if the prediction's fingerprint has moved since the run or if its commit is newer than the flight. Read the report with `<exe> --headless -s scripts/tests/drill_report.gd --path .`, the one instrument that deliberately reads `user://` (one file, the drill's own, written never). Results are **H5 deviation data** like the aim drill's and never enter a base table. `scripts/tests/drill_smoke.gd` is the off-board plumbing test; its numbers are not data.
- **Look pass** (post/atmosphere + emissive), applied game-wide (both greybox and dev_room): a `LookController` (`scripts/environment/look_controller.gd`) applies `LookConfig` (`resources/look_config.gd`, AgX tonemap + glow/SSAO/fog/color-grade/sun) onto the scene's Environment/Sun every frame, live-tuned via the overlay's **LOOK** section. Emissive palette by role (bloom threshold is 1.0, so accents use energy >1): cyan/blue = navigation (gates), red = threat (enemies/turrets), orange = score (targets), amber = course pylons, green = pads, yellow = your fire. Ground is `checker_ground.gdshader` (neon grid); structures use `neon_structure.gdshader` (panel seams). The overlay leaves `look_config` null → LOOK section skipped only where a scene has no LookController.
- Headless verify after edits (treat warnings as errors):
  - Re-import: `<exe> --headless --import --path .`
  - Boot check: `<exe> --headless --quit-after 10 --path .`
  - Script parse check: `<exe> --headless --check-only -s <script.gd> --path .`

## Architecture

- `scenes/` — `menu_tower.tscn` (boot scene: the flyable menu, B5 — `scripts/menu_tower.gd` + `scripts/menu/` for the runtime `MenuBuilding` (a floor stack) built from `BuildingGenerator` (the seeded, deterministic B3/B4 floor-list generator — a save naming a seed names a building), the parametric `MenuFloorFrame` (per-floor enterability: open / sealed / under-construction), the `MenuFloor` commit-on-exit zone, `GlowText3D` primitive neon glyphs, and the `MenuLaunch` cross-scene static; FLY FREE = main with `MenuLaunch.free_fly`), `main.tscn` (the game: environment + drone + combat + UI), `dev_map.tscn` (dev-room testbed, same wiring), `environment/` (greybox, dev_room), `drone/drone.tscn`, `combat/` (projectile, explosion, target, turret), `ui/` (debug_overlay, hud, draft_screen)
- `scripts/` — `drone/` (flight_controller, motor_model, input_handler, rate_controller), `combat/` (weapon, projectile+pool, flak_pod+flak_shell, health, turret, target, enemy_drone, raider_pack — a bench measurement grouping, not wave-spawned, wave_director, effects), `war/` (M6 war campaign: theater_generator + war_sim + war_manifest + sortie_composer — pure static-func modules over a serializable state Dictionary; deterministic, seed-driven; every evolving float goes through `WarSim.quantize` so the state round-trips var_to_str bit-exactly = the portable save — **never `snappedf`, which is not decimal-exact**. The chain is: the sim trades abstract garrison strength → `WarManifest` projects a garrison into a named unit list and prices kills back into strength (`strength_cost` is the exchange rate; a swarm's unit is the pack) → `SortieComposer` turns a node into a `sortie_spec` (archetype/objective, layered garrison, triggered reserves taken OUT of the garrison, approach, pads, dares, and the H.q2 difficulty INPUT vector — **never a difficulty score; SDI is measured by the harness, not authored**). `WarSim.apply_sortie` closes the loop — a FLOWN sortie's result priced back into the node, beside `_proxy_sortie`'s abstract stand-in and sharing its outcome vocabulary so the harness and the game cannot calibrate different things; `WarSave` is F4's portable file. Benches: `tests/war_soak.gd` invariant soak, `tests/war_trace.gd` single-war trace, `tests/sortie_trace.gd` composed-sortie trace; checks: `tests/manifest_check.gd`, `tests/sortie_compose_check.gd`, `tests/war_loop_check.gd`), `sortie/` (**the bridge**: `sortie_runner.gd` turns a `sortie_spec` into a fight — concentric layer placement, `ObjectiveAsset` structures, triggered reserves, egress, and a serializable result the war eats via `WarManifest.dent_from_kills`. The ONLY place a spec becomes a Node3D, which is what keeps `war/` pure), `warroom/` (P1.8's between-sorties screen: `war_view.gd` derives the whole map as pure functions over the war state — hex projection, front line, supply edges, and the per-node refusal that answers "can this be flown"; `hex_table.gd` renders it; `war_room.gd` is the scene. `WarSim.supplied_set` and `WarSim.strike_range` are public **for this** — a map that computed its own reach would eventually promise ground the campaign does not have), `audio/` (sound_bank — synthesized, no external assets; motor/wind emitters), `ui/`, `tests/` (headless checks: hover, combat, wave). The drone's `Blackbox` records per-tick flight CSVs under `user://blackbox/` plus a sparse `events_*.csv` companion (one line per shot/hit/spawn/kill/wave via the null-safe static `Blackbox.log_event`, SoundBank-style) — both open on arm, close on disarm, disabled headless
- `resources/` — `tunable_config.gd` base + `flight_config.gd`/`frame_config.gd`/`combat_config.gd`/`damage_config.gd`/`enemy_config.gd` and their `default_*.tres` (shared instances: every exporter of the same `.tres` sees live edits), shared shaders/materials. **`EnemyConfig` is one class with many instances** (`default_enemy_<type>.tres`, one per bestiary type per GAMEPLAY-DESIGN P4.8), so its save/defaults paths derive from `type_id` instead of being class constants; **`FrameConfig` and `FlightConfig` are the same** (`default_frame_<id>.tres` + `default_flight_<id>.tres`, paths derived from `frame_id`) — a frame carries its own flight model (P3.9: frames ARE flight configs) plus hull/armor, and `FlightController` applies both in `_ready`; `CombatConfig` is player-side only. Configs with many instances list their id field in `identity_fields()` so a preset loaded across instances cannot rename one.
- Combat wiring: projectiles call `take_hit(damage)` on whatever they hit unless it shares the shooter's `team`; entities award score via a `destroyed(points)` signal that `main.gd` tallies; `SoundBank` is a scene node with a null-safe **static** API (not an autoload — autoloads don't exist under `--script` test runs).
- **Electronic warfare** is one scalar (`scripts/combat/jamming.gd`, `Jamming.level_at(node)` → 0 clean, 1 fully jammed) read by four consumers: the gun director's solution window (`Weapon.director_window`), the missile lock's build rate (refused outright at 1.0), the flak fuse radius (baked at launch — the ranging is onboard), and the video-feed breakup, which reuses the battle-damage overlay so EW and damage read as one failure (D6). Emitters join the `jammers` group and own their own falloff shape (`jam_level_at`); the Screamer is the only one today. `Jamming.bench_override` lets a bench STATE its jam the way delivery cells state their jink.
- **Motor audio is FOUR emitters, one per rotor** (`scripts/audio/motor_audio.gd`), placed at the real mounts and rebuilt on `FlightController.frame_changed` — so a damaged rotor, an asymmetric hover and the airframe's own stereo width are all audible. Level is driven by **thrust in g** (`motor_output x TWR`, so hover = 1.0 on every frame), never by stick position: motor output is a *fraction of maximum* thrust, and hover sits at 1/TWR, which made the TWR-12 frames quieter than the Kestrel while moving 500 kg. `unit_size` is `body_m x 4` for the same reason — a fixed 10 m put the Roc 15.6 dB below the Kestrel in chase view. Tone is **baked per frame** (420/163/90 Hz for kestrel/condor/roc) rather than pitch-shifted, with a sub-octave fading in and the octave-up fading out as size rises. Wind scales to `FlightConfig.terminal_speed()`. **A constant that was correct for one airframe is a bug on a size ladder** — that has now bitten three times (overlay slider ranges, wind saturation, audio unit_size).
- Physics tick is **240 Hz** (rate-controller stability); all flight code runs on the fixed tick.
- **Pilot axis convention** for all Vector3 rate/gain fields: x = roll (+right), y = pitch (+nose up), z = yaw (+right). Drone front is body **-Z**. Conversion from Godot space happens only in `FlightController._measured_rates()`.

## Conventions

- **BREAKING A SAVED WAR IS ALLOWED, AND PREFERRED OVER SLOWING DEVELOPMENT** (user's rule, 2026-08-05, and it is a standing one). Do **not** design around, defer, or complicate a change to keep an in-progress `user://war.save` loadable. Bump `SAVE_VERSION`, change the state shape, rename fields — the human starts a new war and does not mind: *"i can start a new game over and over and over again, no problems. the development should take priority."* A campaign is meaningless a few iterations out; the systems are not. This holds **until the game solidifies**, at which point it gets revisited — until then, prefer the break.
  - **The one thing this does NOT license**: a test or a bench *accidentally* destroying the human's data as a side effect of measuring something else. Deleting a save because the schema moved on is progress; deleting it because a check borrowed the file and crashed is a bug. Keep `user://` writes out of the harness (`PlayerProfile.save()` and `WarSave.PATH` are the two precedents).
- **No magic numbers in physics/input code** — every tunable is an `@export` in `FlightConfig`; scripts read it, the debug overlay writes it live.
- Static typing everywhere; `snake_case`; short focused functions; comments explain *why*, not *what*.
- Composition over inheritance; scenes minimal; `.tscn`/`.tres`/`project.godot` are hand-edited text — keep them small and diff-readable.
- Hand-written `.tscn` gotcha: exported Node properties need `node_paths=PackedStringArray("prop")` on the `[node]` line **and** `prop = NodePath("...")` in its body, or the reference silently loads as Nil.
- Never touch `.godot/`. No third-party addons or external assets without asking — greybox primitives and flat/procedural materials only.
- Explain non-obvious reasoning briefly; flag uncertainty instead of guessing silently.

## Controls & tuning

- Gamepad (Mode 2): left stick throttle/yaw, right stick pitch/roll. **A** arm (throttle at zero-thrust position), **B** reset, **Y** acro/angle mode, **X** FPV/chase camera, **RT** fire blaster, **LT** fire missile (requires lock: hold an enemy near the reticle until the diamond turns red), **RB** fire flak pod (proximity-fused burst; no lock, no director — point and hold), **Start** debug overlay. Keyboard fallbacks ship by default (Enter arm, R reset, M mode, C camera, Space fire, F missile, G flak, Tab overlay).
- **Input system**: `input_profile` dropdown (top of overlay FLIGHT) selects gamepad vs USB radio (EdgeTX joystick mode, e.g. RadioMaster TX16S; AETR/TAER channel orders on axes 0–3; auto-detected by joypad name, gamepad fallback; wrong-direction axes get flipped on the radio's Outputs page). Buttons/switches bind via the overlay's **BINDINGS** section: `InputBindings` (`resources/input_bindings.gd`) rewrites the InputMap at runtime — press-to-bind captures keys, joypad buttons, **and axes as switches** (how radio switches arrive). `arm_switch` is stateful FPV arming (switch position = armed), unbound by default; `arm_toggle` stays momentary; `missile_auto_switch` (also stateful, unbound) is the missile director — with it on, a stable full lock auto-launches after `missile_auto_hold_s`. The blaster's gun director is `fire_assist_miss_m` (CombatConfig; 0 = off). Persists to `user://input_bindings.tres` via the normal Save/Load/Defaults.
- **Pause mode** (BeamNG-style): `pause_toggle` (default **P**) / `pause_switch` slow time to `pause_time_scale` (FlightConfig) instead of stopping; the drone's autopilot holds position (level-and-brake + hover collective, `autopilot_*` tunables) and bindings swap to the **paused context** (second mapping set, gameplay actions unbound by default — safe typing in overlay fields; edit via the BINDINGS context dropdown). Pause/overlay actions always stay bound from the flight set.
- **WEATHER (todo)** overlay section: `WeatherConfig` stub (wind/precipitation/fog/heat fields, persisted but not yet simulated) — placeholder for GAMEPLAY-DESIGN P1.6.

- **The feasibility bench** (`scripts/tests/swarm_bench.gd`, Iteration 16 / L13 phase 0.1) is where the ENGINE's limits are metered rather than the balance's: table A sweeps unit counts and reports the physics-tick capacity, table B puts N raiders against one hovering frame of each size. **Wall-clock per tick is NOT a valid load instrument** — Godot paces physics to real time and waits when there is headroom, so it reads 4.169 ms at every unit count; the working measure is to raise `Engine.physics_ticks_per_second` far above 240 and read how many ticks the machine actually delivers. It deliberately never measures the heavy frame shooting BACK (pilot v7 is Kestrel-tuned, so that number would be a confound, not a result) and its target never evades, so table B is the swarm's ceiling.
- **Balance instrument** (GAMEPLAY-DESIGN v1.23/v1.24, symmetric half added Iteration 9) — read [BALANCE.md](BALANCE.md) before touching it or acting on its output. - Rate-loop wobble is fought with the FlightConfig **Filtering** group (gyro LPF, D-term LPF, RC smoothing — Betaflight-style), never by changing the physics.
- Rate feel is a `rate_preset` dropdown at the top of the overlay's FLIGHT section (`FlightPresets`, `scripts/drone/flight_presets.gd`): Default/Cinematic/Freestyle/Race, each an atomic snapshot of rate_p/i/d/ff + angular_damping. Hand-tuning any of those five fields falls through to "Custom" — detected live by comparing the config against every preset, not tracked as separate state. Bench-tune new presets with `scripts/tests/rate_tune_sweep.gd` and `step_response.gd` before adding them.
- **Ammunition** (Iteration 10, steered R.q1-R.q6): the blaster is the FLOOR and never runs out — what it has is a **heat sink** (`CombatConfig` Heat subgroup: fills per bolt, vents only after quiet, latches an overheat lockout until `heat_reset_fraction`), so it paces you rather than stranding you. The **flak pod and missile rack carry magazines** and are refilled two ways: a fly-through `ResupplyGate` (a **solid** four-bar frame like `environment/gate.tscn`, so clipping it costs you; amber = flak, violet = missile; finite `charges`, labelled INSIDE the opening with the menu tower's `GlowText3D` glyphs, and laid per sortie by the wave director via rejection sampling — separated from each other and from scenery, fewer as the sortie number rises, which is P2.6's pad-poor knob), and a `Salvage` drop from any kill (R.q6: the reason to bother killing the respawning arena turrets) — **a cleared wave re-arms nothing** (R.q3 retracted v1.93 after play: a free refill made the gates slack). Launchers join the `magazines` group and expose `ammo_kind`/`rounds`/`magazine()`/`rearm()`, so a gate or a drop never needs to know what a drone is built from. A magazine of **0 means unlimited**, which is how the whole mechanic switches off from a config.
- Run flow (M4): arming starts a run of sorties (`sortie_waves` waves each); clearing a sortie opens the exit gate, and flying it triggers a paused upgrade draft applied to the run-scoped `RunMods` static layer (never to the configs). Death ends the run, records `user://profile.json`, shows the summary; the next arm starts fresh (`RunMods.reset()`). Score is run-scoped with a combo multiplier.
- **Waves are COMPOSED, not counted** (v1.85): `WaveDirector.ROSTER` (type → scene + placement + the escort flag) and `WaveDirector.PLAN` (sortie × wave → the named units this wave spends its budget on) are the whole of it — **adding a bestiary type to the run is a ROSTER row plus a PLAN slot, never new code**. Named units come **out of** the budget, raiders fill the rest (SortieComposer's reserve discipline, P2.3 — the war sim itself is deliberately *not* wired into M4). The unit is the unit (P4.q5): `remaining` counts units, so a gnat cloud is 1 and is gone on `cleared`; types that leave the field without dying (an aegis reaching its target) end their unit too, or the wave never clears. The escort rule is enforced in `compose()`, not just stated: a screamer carries no weapon, so a wave must always hold something that threatens. `composition_check.gd` covers all of it.

## Reporting back to the human

**Use the `report-back` skill (`.claude/skills/report-back/`) whenever you summarise findings, results, a diagnosis, a measurement or completed work** — Goal / Terms / What I found / What's next. The **Terms** section is the point: working alone you coin vocabulary (`sight-clamp`, `cleared fraction`, `dent 9.7`) that reads as ordinary to you within minutes and is a private word to the human. A report they cannot fully parse cannot be argued with, and their disagreement is the most valuable thing they give you — so define every term they have not used themselves, and give every raw bench number its unit, its scale and its meaning. Length is not the cost to optimise; comprehension is (the user's call, 2026-08-03).

**Say how long a long run will take, before starting it** (user's rule, 2026-08-15): *"when you launch a certain run where it may run for minutes AND you have the ability to estimate how much time it would take, i feel it would be useful if you can just mention the estimation at the session."* One clause is enough — "the full board, about 5 minutes". A command with no stated duration is indistinguishable from a hang. Measured here: the full 27-check board **~4-5 min** (dominated by `lethality_check`), `lethality_check` alone **~8 min**, the other 26 **~2-3 min**, `swarm_bench` **~2 min**, `tunnel_check` and `city_load_bench` **~1 min each**, `drill_smoke` **~40 s per drill** (it spends real seconds, because Godot paces physics to wall-clock). Single checks, `--import` and boot checks are seconds and need no estimate. Only estimate when you actually can; say "unknown" rather than invent one.

## Checkpoint protocol

Work is phased (handoff §11). **At each phase checkpoint: stop, summarize what changed and why, and wait for the human to run/fly it.** Flight feel cannot be evaluated by the agent — the human's hands are the test suite. Map feel feedback to config changes per handoff §14.
